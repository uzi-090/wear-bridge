# Pairing and device removal

Implemented 7 October 2026. Both apps have a Device screen for pairing, unpairing and forgetting the saved device.

| State | Device features and data | Reconnection |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unpaired | Hidden; only setup, device management and help are available | Disabled until Pair is tapped |
| Pairing / awaiting approval | Hidden; the approval code and setup guidance are available | Setup only |
| Paired, disconnected | Hidden, including cached battery, notification counts, contacts, player information and feature detail screens | Automatic when Bluetooth returns and the device is nearby |
| Paired, connected | Available after approval and a working connection | Enabled |
| Unpairing | Hidden immediately; queued feature work is invalidated | Disabled |

On the watch, Pair iPhone starts advertising. On the iPhone, Pair Watch lists nearby Bridge watches and refreshes system-connected candidates every two seconds while searching, so the user chooses the intended device. Pair Watch Again, Try Again and Reconnect Watch run the same search and select the saved watch automatically when it connects or advertises; if it has a new identifier (after Forget in Bluetooth settings), the user picks it from the list. This also prevents an old watch left advertising after an offline Forget from automatically winning replacement setup. Confirm the Bluetooth prompt and approve the Bridge code on the watch. Existing approved installations migrate without deleting their data, but features stay hidden until the connection is ready. A fresh approval request changes the iPhone state to Pairing even for a previously saved watch.

While paired, the watch only accepts its saved iPhone. During setup (Pair iPhone / Pair again) it accepts any iPhone, because a phone that bonds again arrives with a new private Bluetooth address; the matching-code approval decides, and the approved phone's address replaces the saved one. The watch trusts one iPhone app key at a time. A new key asking for approval revokes the old one at once, and approving it clears data synced for the previous relationship. An unanswered approval expires after two minutes (sent to the iPhone as declined, with Ask Again).

After Forget, the saved watch identifier/phone address is absent. A subsequent Pair starts new discovery/advertising, requires fresh app approval, and saves only the newly approved device. Synced contacts, app rules, icons and notification history from the forgotten peer are cleared before replacement setup. A persisted cleanup journal resumes interrupted removal on startup; Forget removes the watch's saved phone address when removal begins. Failed cleanup blocks pairing until retry succeeds. The iPhone independently tracks data cleanup and identity replacement, so completing one cannot skip the other after restart. Late icon downloads cannot repopulate the removed device's cache. The watch rejects commands/subscriptions from a second central while a device connection is active. Interrupted removal stays unpaired even if old trust remains on disk; a late removal timer or approval cannot complete or re-enable a later pairing attempt.

Unpair clears app approval, synced contacts and contact selections, notification history and action state, app rules/catalogs/icons, live battery/model/media data and Find/call alerts. It retains the saved device entry. Forget performs that cleanup and also removes the entry. Neither action changes the iPhone's source contacts or source notifications. The watch clears its encrypted contacts generation file and interrupted temporary file. The iPhone replaces its app identity so an offline watch cannot silently grant the old app approval on a later setup; a persisted reset marker covers interrupted removal and failed Keychain writes.

Connected removal uses a sealed lifecycle request and acknowledgement. The recipient disables features and clears its data before acknowledging. The initiator waits up to five seconds for confirmation; a timeout completes local removal and displays that the other device could not confirm. Offline removal cannot reach the other device: unpair in Bridge there too. This is stated in both confirmations and removal guidance. These messages require current authentication; stale peripheral callbacks and late icon/contact/notification work cannot restore device content.

The watch's service and boot recovery stay stopped after unpairing. Device removal also works if the service is unavailable because background startup was blocked. Pairing and connectivity are separate persistent states; a radio disconnection retains approval, while removal disables reconnect across relaunch/reboot. Open feature navigation stacks are discarded on loss of availability. Watch tile controls and call screens follow the same gate; offline tiles show pairing status instead of saved notification counts.

## Cancelling setup

The iPhone shows Cancel Pairing whenever setup has started but the watch hasn't approved the app (searching, connecting, Bluetooth pairing, waiting for or declined approval, failed attempt). It stops the attempt, keeps a previously saved watch but not one this attempt added, and replaces the app key so a code still shown on the watch can't pair it later. Onboarding Back on the pairing step does the same. If a newly chosen watch disconnects before its Bluetooth pairing completes, the iPhone shows Try Again instead of Connecting… indefinitely. On the watch, Cancel pairing reports "Pairing cancelled" (not an unconfirmed removal), and Forget on an already unpaired watch reports that the saved iPhone was removed.

## System Bluetooth bond

Forgetting the system pairing in Bluetooth settings (on either device) is not an app Unpair. The watch keeps the approved app key, drops the authenticated session, and advertises for discovery; the iPhone app keeps its approval and, when iOS reports that the watch removed its pairing information, explains Forget This Device and Try Again. After the fresh system pairing the trusted key reconnects without a new approval, and the new address/identifier replaces the saved one on both sides.

The controls remove the **Bridge app relationship**. Removing the OS Bluetooth bond still requires Forget/Unpair in Bluetooth Settings on each device. This app does not use private/reflected bond-removal APIs. This boundary follows the public [Core Bluetooth central API](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corebluetooth/cbcentralmanager) and [Android BluetoothDevice API](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/bluetooth/BluetoothDevice); the current watch uses ordinary GATT bonding, not a Companion Device Manager association. Both Device screens explain the Settings step; the watch includes a Bluetooth Settings shortcut.
